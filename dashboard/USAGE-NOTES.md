# Reading the radar as a workflow

This note describes a repeatable way to use the board. It does not assume any
prior knowledge of the protocols and it does not promise a reward.

Start by fixing the snapshot date in your notes. Every figure on this page is
relative to that date. A board read weeks later is a different board.

Decide what you are looking for before sorting. A large, established protocol
and a small, fast-growing one answer different questions. The board supports
both, but only if you know which one you are asking about.

Read the top row first. Note its TVL, its weekly change, its age and its number
of chains. Then read the second row. The difference between them is explained
by exactly those four inputs and the constants printed in the model panel.

Use the detail view on one row. Confirm that the recomputed value equals the
board value. This single check tells you whether the page you are looking at
belongs to the data it displays.

Only after that should you look at the farm plan. The plan is generic by
design: it depends on category, size, age and chain, not on the protocol's name.
A reader can therefore apply the same reasoning to every row.

Write down the suggested capital and the holding period before opening any
application. A plan written after the transaction is easier to rationalise.

Check the chain names against the protocol's own site. A protocol may be live
on a chain through a third-party bridge, and the action in the plan assumes a
canonical route.

If the weekly change is extreme, look for the cause. Incentive programmes can
inflate TVL for a few days. The model bounds the effect, but the underlying
fact still matters.

If the age is missing, remember that the model assumed a default. A missing
age is not evidence of youth; it is absence of data.

Compare the implied value with the market cap of a similar live protocol. The
two numbers are not directly comparable, but the comparison is useful context
for the order of magnitude.

Move to the filtered view. Narrow to one category and read the first three
rows. The same model now applies inside a narrower peer group, which makes the
differences easier to interpret.

Switch to the live table. Confirm that those protocols carry a market
capitalisation and that they are not mixed into the pre-token ranking. This is
the separation the dashboard promises.

Return to the board and repeat with a different category. Repetition is how a
reader learns which combinations of inputs produce a high implied value.

Close the tab with a short note: the date, the rows you examined, and whether
any of them justified further research. A short record is more useful than a
long memory.

The board is a tool for ordering public records. It is not a signal, a rating,
or a recommendation. Every figure can be checked against the snapshot, and
every conclusion remains the reader's responsibility.
